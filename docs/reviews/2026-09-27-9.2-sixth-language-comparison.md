**Read-through:** every serious demand candidate (Japanese, Korean, Mandarin,
Arabic) sits in FSI Category IV — the same tier as the most complex scripts —
so "high demand" and "low cost" do not co-occur in this set. The cheapest
adds (Dutch/Swedish, Category I) have the weakest demand signal. The current
five (Cat I/II) were the low-cost quadrant.

## Comparison against "finish the intermediate path in the five"

1. **Authoring cost asymmetry.** The 8.x pipeline authors one unit per gated
   slice in existing Latin-script languages. A Cat-IV language implies: new
   script rendering (furigana/RTL/tashkeel as applicable), new accent-marking
   story, TTS-quality caveats the current provenance/reviewPending pattern
   must extend to, and source traditions with **unverified or commercial
   licensing** (the two verified-free candidates: Korean stdict with unknown
   redistribution terms; Dutch ANS with unverified license). The plan's own
   ledger discipline (two independent sources or an honest gap flag) gets
   harder, not easier, in every Cat-IV candidate researched.
2. **What 9.1 actually changes.** The course registry collapses *shell*
   metadata to one edit — it does not reduce content, script, TTS-review, or
   licensing cost. Anyone sizing Option A should not read 9.1 as that subsidy.
3. **Review debt ordering.** The five shipped languages still carry unsigned-
   off ledgers (native review pending rows 1–48+49–54), open device
   walkthroughs, and the 7.2 self-study gate. Option A adds a sixth unsigned
   curriculum ahead of clearing those — the plan's stop rules sequence
   milestones 8 → 9 explicitly against that.
4. **In favor of Option A (steelman):** demand for Japanese/Korean is real and
   growing; a *private one-unit pilot* (the plan's required shape) is cheap
   relative to a full course and would produce evidence instead of debate;
   script investment (furigana/RTL components) is reusable across units once
   built.

## If the developer chooses one later (plan-mandated shape)

Start with a **private one-unit pilot** and its own design + validation plan —
never a shipped pack. In that plan: verify TTS voice quality on-device first
(provenance record with `reviewPending: true`), resolve source licensing in
writing before authoring, scope the script/input work (furigana, RTL, input
method) as its own engineering slice, and keep the honest-labeling rules
identical to 8.x. Candidate shortlist from this research, by
demand ÷ cost: **Korean** (strong demand, cheap script, free source with
unclear redistribution terms) > **Japanese** (strongest demand, highest
content cost, commercial accent source) > **Russian** (mid demand, mid cost)
> Dutch/Swedish (low cost, weak demand) > Mandarin/Arabic (unverified source
position adds risk beyond FSI-IV cost).
